# Tufte principles

## 1. Show the data

- Put evidence at the center.
- Decorative treatment is acceptable only if it does not compete with the data.

## 2. Maximize data-ink ratio

- Most marks should encode data, not framing.
- Non-data ink must earn its place through orientation, grouping, or explanation.

## 3. Erase chartjunk

- Remove decoration that does not improve comprehension.
- Remove redundant encoding when a simpler one already carries the message.

## 4. Preserve graphical integrity

- Visual effect should match numeric effect.
- Compare position/length before area/volume whenever possible.
- Beware truncated axes, pictograms, 3D perspective, and uneven scaling.

## 5. Encourage comparison

- Most analytical value comes from comparing against something else.
- Use shared scales, aligned baselines, reference lines, small multiples, and sorted order.

## 6. Integrate words, numbers, and images

- Put explanation close to the evidence.
- Prefer direct labels and short captions over detached legends and footnote scavenger hunts.

## 7. Use layering and separation

- Primary data should dominate.
- Secondary structure should recede.
- Context belongs in lighter tone, thinner stroke, or smaller type.

## 8. Reveal micro and macro

- Let a viewer see both the broad pattern and the local detail.
- Sparklines, small multiples, and annotated tables are often better than one oversized summary chart.

## 9. Choose density deliberately

- Dense is good when it stays legible.
- Sparse is good when the task is quick lookup or a single high-stakes comparison.

## 10. Default questions

Before shipping a quantitative display, ask:

- Compared to what?
- What does the viewer need to decide?
- What can be erased?
- What is colliding?
- What is overstated?
- Where should the explanation sit?
